Deconstructing the Wisdom of Crowds

Built a 4.7-million-record quantitative pipeline to evaluate behavioral biases and market calibration in decentralized prediction markets.

Prediction markets are open exchanges where participants trade shares tied to the outcomes of future events, causing asset prices to function as real-time, crowd-sourced probability estimates. While these platforms often display remarkable accuracy in structured domains like sports, other more opaque categories—such as geopolitical conflicts—operate under extreme uncertainty and informational asymmetry. This project tests whether financial incentives move crowds to accurately price geopolitical risk, or if behavioral biases and a lack of information cause the market to systematically misprice probability when facing global crises.

Raw data source: A geopolitical event market interface extracted via the Polymarket API.

Unstructured Data

Processing millions of trades required robust data engineering. Using DuckDB and SQL, the project relies on memory-safe pipelines to query 4.7 million Parquet records efficiently. Because the raw database lacked categorical tags, complex Regex pipelines were built to algorithmically isolate different categories such as geopolitical conflicts, sports, among others.

Solving Missing Data with Blockchain Mechanics

The raw dataset had a critical flaw: it lacked exact timestamps for trades. Instead of discarding the data, an 'API-Free Block Interpolation' method was engineered. By anchoring a known timestamp to a Polygon block number and calculating forward using the average 2.24 second block time, the exact time of every trade was reverse-engineered using pure math and SQL.

The Quantitative Pipeline

Precision and Data Integrity

bar_chart Statistical Rigor

Implemented Logistic Recalibration via Maximum Likelihood Estimation to map the implied market probabilities against real-world frequencies, objectively measuring market confidence.

filter_alt Preventing Data Leaks

Built a 'Dynamic Market Resolution' filter. Instead of relying on arbitrary administrative end dates, SQL was used to track the exact block where the probability permanently collapsed (above 99% or below 1%). This ensured post-resolution trades did not skew the prediction accuracy measurement.

The Psychology of Uncertainty

As geopolitical events near resolution, markets become severely underconfident, artificially compressing probabilities toward 50%. Traders freeze due to extreme informational asymmetry and the "fog of war". Measuring this presents a strict epistemological challenge: raw data cannot natively distinguish a genuine prediction from a leaked decision or a lagging administrative resolution. To isolate true forecasting from late-stage news arbitrage, the trajectory below explicitly filters out post-clarity trades. While this successfully reveals the intense volatility that spikes during genuine uncertainty, it introduces a deliberate analytical trade-off: it inherently understates the market's total predictive efficiency, as pricing in a guaranteed outcome is itself a valid form of prediction. The final dataset will address this constraint.

Reviewing Brain Stimulation for Parkinson's Disease

A data-driven audit of how electrical therapy and biological timing affect movement.

The Baseline Claim

A recent study (Agarwal et al., 2018) tested if mild electrical brain stimulation could improve motor function in Parkinson's patients. The original researchers claimed it was a major, statistically significant success across multiple measures.

Uncovering Flawed Data

By reconstructing the study's math, we found a critical error: the authors quietly excluded a patient who did not respond well to the treatment without declaring it in their methodology. Once that missing data point was correctly restored to the analysis, the alleged 'major success' was no longer statistically significant for the primary outcome measure.

The True Findings

To determine the true clinical efficacy of the intervention, we conducted a strict pre-post statistical replication across both global and fine motor dexterity tests. By re-incorporating the excluded patient data, a much more nuanced picture of neuroplasticity emerged.

The data suggests tDCS benefitted specific fine-motor skills (like using the non-dominant hand), even when the broader motor function claims fell short.

Further Analysis: The Circadian Variable

A deep dive into the temporal data revealed that treatment efficacy is heavily influenced by the time of day it is administered. Stratifying the patient outcomes by session time exposed a clear chronobiological pattern in neuroplasticity.

The biological clock matters: patients who completed their sessions later in the afternoon saw significantly better results compared to morning sessions. Patients completing sessions later in the afternoon showed a strong positive correlation with global motor improvement (r = 0.67, p = 0.034).

The Core Insight: The role of Time

While the original study exaggerated the certainty of its overall success, the underlying data revealed something arguably more important: the brain's internal clock potentially changes the efficacy of tDCS's effects on motor function.

Time of Day

A potential mediator for therapeutic success

Redesigning Digital Choice Architecture for Healthier Food Decisions

A Behavioral Economics approach to delivery app interfaces.

The Convenience of unhealthy choices

Algorithmic salience and high-calorie default settings amplify automatic, hypercaloric food choices. Immediate gratification overtakes long-term nutritional goals.

Identifying the Frictions.

Through 65 surveys and 12 in-depth interviews, we mapped the cognitive fatigue users experience within digital food ecosystems. Our research indicates that time scarcity drives users to rely on automatic, low-effort decision-making, bypassing active nutritional deliberation.

Nudging Better Choices.

By restructuring the digital choice architecture, we leveraged behavioral nudges, specifically defaults and salience, to guide users toward healthier options without restricting their freedom of choice. This involved a systematic redesign of the menu hierarchy and selection flow to minimize cognitive friction for selecting healthier meals.

Salience: Reordering the menu to ensure target options are the most visually immediate, leveraging attention to guide behavior

Defaults: Leveraging user inertia by pre-configuring the healthier outcome

A/B Testing Results: The Impact of Nudges

By simply reordering the menu and setting healthy defaults, the proportion of users choosing nutritionally balanced meals more than doubled. This proves modifying choice architecture is a scalable, low-cost alternative to traditional informational campaigns.

Predicting School Absenteeism.

Using a 546,000-record national database to map educational vulnerability and testing what happens when standard machine learning meets imbalanced, real-world data.

With one in four students missing school regularly, this project aimed to determine if standard machine learning models could reliably flag at-risk youth for targeted educational interventions. By auditing the national Aprender 2023 dataset, the goal was to map the structural causes of absenteeism and test whether certain predictive algorithms can actually identify highly vulnerable populations or if they simply collapse under the weight of skewed social data.

The Socioeconomic Reality.

Before building predictive models, exploring the data revealed that school absenteeism is not distributed randomly, and shows clear structural patterns. 

The geographical distribution of high absenteeism rates shows a clear regional pattern, with the highest concentrations in the south and in Buenos Aires.

A detailed breakdown reveals a consistent gradient: students from lower socioeconomic backgrounds are significantly more likely to accumulate high numbers of absences compared to their high-income peers.

Distribution of Language performance levels according to socioeconomic level show a higher prevalence of low scores in the low SES areas.

The Machine Learning Audit.

We wanted to see if we could automate the identification of at-risk students based on their profiles. We ran the dataset through several standard machine learning models, including K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN), Random Forest, and Bagging. We also applied Boosting techniques, but they yielded no improvement. The goal was to see if an algorithm could accurately flag students as having low, medium, or high absentee risk.

The Limits of Out-of-the-Box Prediction.

The models exposed a classic challenge with real-world social data: severe class imbalance. Because 50% of the dataset represented low absenteeism and only 15.8% represented the highly vulnerable minority, the algorithms took the path of least resistance. Even advanced ensemble methods collapsed under the skew, optimizing for overall accuracy rather than minority detection.

KNN Confusion Matrix

The KNN model played it safe, guessing the average outcome for almost everyone and missing the extreme cases.

Bagging Confusion Matrix

The Bagging model successfully identified the low-risk majority but failed to spot the highly vulnerable minority.

Engineering for Structural Realities.

This audit showed that identifying vulnerable students requires more than standard predictive modeling. Because extreme absenteeism is a structural issue driven by socioeconomic barriers, accurate prediction requires engineering richer, localized variables and deploying specific data-balancing techniques (like SMOTE) to ensure the algoritm correctly identifies the students most at risk.

Strava: Platform
Economics & Disruption

A strategic case study evaluating Strava's evolution from a niche fitness-tracking community into a central digital health orchestrator, examining its network moats and the hybrid innovation model necessary to survive the rapid rise of predictive AI analytics

The Disruption Threat.

Strava won the fitness market by acting as a hardware-agnostic orchestrator, capturing the high-margin social layer while hardware complementors absorbed R&D costs. However, the rapid convergence of wellness data and predictive AI analytics represents a "technological discontinuity".

The ecosystem is entering a volatile era of ferment. Without aggressive integration of advanced AI, Strava risks losing its market dominance to faster-moving competitors offering superior, prescriptive health insights.

Analyzing Strava's Network Architecture

Advantage Execution & Orchestration
Interoperability Ledger Acts as the central interoperability ledger, unifying fragmented data across competitors (e.g., Apple, Garmin) and software alliances (e.g., MyFitnessPal, Swift).
Asymmetric Network Effects Leverages a freemium model where indirect network externalities create insurmountable switching costs for users, locking in the community of over 100 million users.
Hardware Agnosticism Extracts value from advanced biosensor data (smartwatches, trackers) without the capital expenditure of physical product manufacturing.
